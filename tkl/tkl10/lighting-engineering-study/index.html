What the ZMK screenshot actually estimates

The genuine nice!nano v2 profile uses 3.3 V. With white LEDs set to 3.2 V and 100 Ω, the calculator assigns 1 mA to each LED, or 6 mA while awake. A 50% sleep setting halves that backlight load.

Recommended architecture

86 single-colour white LEDs, individual ballast resistors, a separate regulated lighting supply and one common PWM control. Put each emitter at the switch manufacturer’s LED position. Orient its optical window toward the actual XVX legend; confirm view/handedness before rotating footprints.

LEDs and assembly

First bottom-side candidate: Lite-On LTW-C230DS, the existing JLC C575513 reverse-mount white LED. Move to one per key and test its low-current output. This keeps bottom-only assembly possible, but 86 reverse placements and routed windows need a new quote and fit/routing checks.

Low-current comparison: Kingbright APT1608LQWF/D, 0603, 1.6 × 0.8 × 0.75 mm, characterized at 2 mA. It is a top-emitting device: use the correct mount, and allow for top-side assembly and switch-cavity verification. Its low-current specification does not prove it is the most efficient choice at 0.08 mA.

Buy/test samples before locking the LED part. Compare useful legend brightness at the same measured battery input power, including colour-bin consistency and the real Whale/XVX combination.

Supply and control

Candidate regulator: TI TPS63900, set to a provisional 3.6 V lighting rail from protected, switched battery power. Keep that load off the clone’s VCC. Its low quiescent current and programmable input limit make it worth evaluating at these loads.

Retain a common low-side MOSFET and BL_EN (P0.31). Give every LED its own resistor. Choose final resistors from measured LED forward voltage, targeting roughly 0.5 mA peak initially; 16% and 40% PWM would then average 0.08 and 0.2 mA. This is a ballasted voltage drive, not 86 precise constant-current sinks.

Prototype resistor options: 1.0, 1.5, 2.2 and 3.3 kΩ; these are a test range, not approved production values. Check current spread and trim groups if needed. Verify regulator transients, input limiting, low battery, MOSFET leakage and the existing 300 mA battery switch/charging path.

Do not PWM the buck-boost converter’s enable pin at the LED dimming frequency. PWM the LED bank; manage regulator shutdown separately if needed. The existing one-PWM-pin approach avoids consuming more MCU pins. An I²C driver would require a GPIO/matrix redesign.

A proper guide prototype

A frosted plate around the switches mostly illuminates the gaps. For a guide to light the legends, its extracted light must enter each switch’s native LED channel. The comparison design therefore places a guide below the PCB, with 86 matching optical windows in a future PCB, rather than assuming it can shine through opaque switch bases.

24-source guide concept

  • Four side-emitting white sources per physical row region, six regions. This is a starting test count, not an established optimum.
  • Candidate source family: Everlight BL-3806, side-view white, with 3.8 × 1.0 × 0.6 mm catalogue example. Obtain the exact suffix’s current datasheet and source availability before PCB layout.
  • Clear PMMA with polished input edges; extraction only beneath the 86 output windows. Increase extraction away from source positions, then tune from measurements.
  • Opaque mask above, white insulating reflector below with controlled spacing. Provide noncontact clearances around switch pins, solder joints, components and supports.
  • Target a 0.8–1.0 mm guide if the stack permits it. The 1.5 mm sample is an optical comparison only. No full-keyboard fit is claimed.
  • A side-lit guide could use four strings of six LEDs with two dual-string boost drivers, such as TPS61162A, sharing a PWM command. Exact current programming, low-load efficiency, package/assembly cost and firmware timing remain to be qualified.

Bench acceptance before a full PCB revision

  1. Check the actual cap and switch. Confirm the XVX legend transmits light and identify the Whale’s LED channel/orientation. Test 1U and wide caps at rest and full 2.8 mm travel.
  2. Compare useful brightness at equal battery power. Eight direct emitters versus two opposed side emitters feeding the eight-window guide. Measure both LED supply power and converter input power; different LED forward voltages make equal current an unfair comparison.
  3. Use the guide’s opaque mask. No light outside the eight output windows should count toward legend brightness. Test sparse, medium and dense patterns with the same reflector/input alignment.
  4. Targets to verify: comfortably readable legends in the intended dim room; darkest comparable legend at least half as bright as the brightest; no bare-LED glare; no travel interference. A 5–15 cd/m² legend luminance is an initial night-use tuning target, not a verified product specification.
  5. Power target: try to pass at ≤10 mA incremental battery draw for the full keyboard. If that fails, measure the current that does pass and accept the calculator’s shorter runtime. Also measure lighting-off leakage and the clone’s actual active/sleep consumption.
  6. Then route the next revision. Keep V10 intact; retain the outline, mounts, module and battery where possible. The guide option needs explicit underside clearance modelling. Recheck DRC, native LED apertures, antenna clearance, switch/diode routing, 300 mA switch path and firmware.

A calibrated luminance meter can verify cd/m². A fixed-exposure linear RAW camera can compare relative uniformity; automatic phone exposure or a beautiful render cannot establish the absolute target.

Firmware starting point and references

Use one monochrome backlight channel, no animated RGB. Start at low duty, provide brightness keys, and turn the lighting off after 30 seconds idle. Stock ZMK supports backlight idle-off; battery-dependent brightness caps/fading need additional behavior if wanted.
