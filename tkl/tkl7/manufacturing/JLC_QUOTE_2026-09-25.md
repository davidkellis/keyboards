## Economic assembly tested and rejected for the complete BOM

The site accepted the PCB construction and quoted $148.01 fabrication without
Standard assembly rails. At BOM matching, however, U1 **NRF52840-QIAA-R7 /
C1851953** was explicitly marked **Standard Only**. The site's dialog offered
switching to Standard or leaving that controller off the board. We switched
to Standard and verified that all 31 groups, including U1 and J3, were selected.

This is a part-specific service restriction; the generic dialog did not
establish its precise manufacturing reason. Single-sided SMT/THT, board size
and layer count alone are not the blocker. No incomplete Economic total is
presented as the cost of an assembled keyboard.

## Further cost targets

1. The existing design still incurs **$22.60 small-via + $19.77 Kelvin** fees.
   The PCB note requests review against the actual 0.20 mm holes / 0.45 mm
   pads. A combined $42.37 reduction is only a conditional target; neither
   fee has been waived. Do not select false drill dimensions or drop via fill.
2. Five boards consume **$39.672 of BAT46W-7-F diodes**, the largest component
   line, ahead of **$30.327 for the five controllers**. A cheaper diode is a
   worthwhile design study, but it must preserve the matrix's 1.8 V input-low
   margin, leakage and footprint. No replacement is approved or substituted.
3. For a larger redesign, investigate a controller or radio module that JLC
   explicitly accepts for Economic assembly. Reducing RF part types and
   avoiding fine-pitch via-in-pad routing may reduce several charges. This
   is an engineering hypothesis, not a priced or validated alternative.
4. Basic-part substitutions alone do not remove Standard feeder charges:
   Standard charges for both basic and extended parts. Reducing distinct BOM
   groups is the relevant target. Review RF passives and crystals carefully.
